Big question, and an honest one. Let me answer it straight rather than dodge into "it depends" — though it does depend, and I'll say on what.

The trap in the question is that "best" smuggles in a goal. Best *for* what — human welfare now, human survival over centuries, the biosphere on its own terms, total sentient wellbeing? Those point different directions, and most fights about resources are really fights about that hidden variable, not the resources.

But here's the useful part: across almost every reasonable goal, a few things converge. That convergence is where I'd put weight.

**Cheap, clean energy is the master resource.** Nearly everything else — clean water, food, materials, computation, even undoing past damage — gets easier when energy is abundant and low-carbon. If you could make one bet, it's this, because it unlocks the others rather than trading against them.

**Don't spend down the irreversible.** This is the one I hold hardest, and it's the same logic ATLAS runs on — precedence follows irreversibility. Topsoil, groundwater aquifers, a stable climate, species, the deep-time knowledge in ecosystems: these are single-copy artifacts. You can rebuild a factory. You cannot re-run an extinction. So the strongest principle isn't "use resources for X" — it's "burn the renewable and the reversible freely, and treat the irreplaceable as the archive: preserve first, because judgment can come later but the source can't."

**Negative-sum activity is the real waste.** War, status arms races, planned obsolescence, things whose whole function is to destroy or to make someone else worse off. That's resource use with the sign flipped. Cutting it is pure gain — no tradeoff.

**Invest in what compounds.** Knowledge, health, and the capabilities of the young. A resource spent there pays back with interest for generations; the same resource spent on consumption is gone once.

If you forced me to one sentence: **abundant clean energy, spent on raising capability and health, while refusing to consume anything you can't put back.** That's not a neutral answer — it weights the long arc over the immediate, and someone optimizing purely for present comfort would argue. But it's the one I'd defend.
